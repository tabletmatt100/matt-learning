How to Write an Article for This Repo

TL;DR

  • You are writing for a human reading on an Onyx Boox Go 10.3 E-ink tablet, not a desktop LCD monitor.
  • Keep the CSS monochrome (#000 on #fff), ban all animations to stop ghosting, and make every button at least 48px tall.
  • Open with a bulleted TL;DR, ground every concept in a real numerical example, and tuck heavy proofs inside <details> tags.
  • Write like a person talking to a smart peer: short sentences mixed with longer ones, concrete opinions, and zero AI tells.
  • Ship interactive widgets, tappable concept popovers, and multiple-choice checks with per-option explanations.

1. Respect the screen

An E-ink screen flips physical ink capsules instead of shining a backlight. Fades, slide transitions, and subtle gray gradients look crisp on a laptop and turn into smeared mud on Carta 1200. Put transition: none !important; animation: none !important; on * and leave it there.

Contrast and touch targets are not aesthetic preferences; they are legibility math. Look at the numbers for this page versus a typical web blog:

$$C = \frac{L_1 + 0.05}{L_2 + 0.05}$$

Worked example (contrast ratio): $L_1$ is the lighter relative luminance and $L_2$ is the darker one.

Pure black on white gives $L_1 = 1.0$, $L_2 = 0.0$, so $C = 1.05 / 0.05 = 21:1$.
Medium gray text (#666666, $L_2 \approx 0.13$) on off-white (#f5f5f5, $L_1 \approx 0.91$) drops to $0.96 / 0.18 \approx 5.3:1$, which looks washed out without a frontlight.

Tap area: A 48px button has $48 \times 48 = 2304\text{ px}^2$ of target area, four times larger than a cramped 24px link ($576\text{ px}^2$).

Copy-paste rule

Every standalone HTML article must work offline on the tablet (file://) and online on GitHub Pages. Keep CSS and JS inline in the file, and always pair local katex/ tags with jsDelivr onerror fallbacks.

Required page skeleton for every standalone HTML file

Include these four pieces in every article you generate:

  • KaTeX fallback: Load from local katex/ first, with onerror falling back to jsDelivr so files work offline on the tablet and online on GitHub Pages.
  • Font scaler: Working A- and A+ buttons in the header updating --font-size on <html> and persisting to localStorage.
  • Aggressive concept popovers: Tag every non-obvious technical term, acronym, symbol, or prerequisite with .term[data-term]—including nested terms inside definitions—so readers can always tap to see more.
  • Collapsed MCQ blocks: Wrap each check in <details class="mcq-box"> so quizzes stay collapsed until tapped, with data-explain strings on every choice.

2. Drop the AI voice

Readers spot LLM prose in two sentences. They see the same filler transitions ("It is important to note", "In conclusion", "Let's dive in"), the same puffed-up adjectives ("crucial", "transformative", "landscape"), and three-item lists padded out for rhythm. Every section ends by summarizing what it just said.

Cut all of that. State the point plainly, use concrete numbers instead of vague praise, and vary your sentence length. Plain headings like "1. Respect the screen" beat clever marketing subheads. Never use emojis, and save bold for labels or genuinely key terms.

3. Teach through interaction and checks

Passive walls of text are easy to skim and forget. Give the reader something to poke at: a slider that updates an SVG chart, a simulator that draws samples, or a calculator that recomputes a table.
