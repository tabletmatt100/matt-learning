Hierarchical Multiclass Classification: Taxonomy Losses, Chain-Rule Routing & Rollup

TL;DR

  • Why flat cross-entropy fails on trees: Standard $K$-way softmax treats every wrong leaf as equally wrong (tree distance ignored). Worse, picking the highest-probability leaf (leaf argmax) lets an unsplit class win even when two sibling leaves share $60\%$ of the total mass on a rival branch.
  • Chain-rule factorization vs. leaf rollup: Along any root-to-leaf path $\text{Anc}(y)$, a leaf's probability is the product of local parent-to-child conditionals: $P(y) = \prod_{v \in \text{Anc}(y)} P(v \mid \text{pa}(v))$. In reverse, a parent node's probability is the exact sum of its descendant leaves (bottom-up rollup): $P(u) = \sum_{\ell \in \text{Leaves}(u)} P(\ell)$.
  • Stopping at an internal node (selective abstention): When leaf uncertainty is high but parent branch mass $P(u)$ is high, backing off to the parent node avoids a high-cost leaf guess while preserving actionable hierarchical precision and recall ($hF_1$).
  • Where top-down routing breaks: Greedy top-down routing commits to the winning child at level 1 and prunes all other branches forever (error propagation). Either sum/multiply full path probabilities across the tree or decode with beam search.

2. Chain-rule factorization vs. bottom-up rollup

In a strict tree taxonomy, every leaf $y$ has a unique path of ancestors $\text{Anc}(y)$ from the root. That tree structure gives you two mathematically equivalent ways to couple parent and leaf probabilities so they stay 100% consistent:

  1. Top-Down Conditional Factorization (Local Classifiers): Train a small softmax at each parent node $\text{pa}(v)$ over its immediate children. By the chain rule of probability, a leaf's probability is the product of conditionals along its root-to-leaf path, and the total loss is the sum of local cross-entropies along that path.
  2. Bottom-Up Marginal Rollup (Flat Leaf Head + Sum): Keep a single flat softmax over all $L$ leaves, and define every internal parent node's probability as the exact sum of its descendant leaves: $P(u) = \sum_{\ell \in \text{Leaves}(u)} P(\ell)$.

Conditional product $\prod_{v \in \text{Anc}(y)} P(v \mid \text{pa}(v))$ Guarantees every parent $P(u)$ equals the sum of its children and all leaves sum to $1$ If you train independent per-node sigmoids without conditioning, a child can score $P(\text{Pug}) = 0.80$ while its parent scores $P(\text{Dog}) = 0.20$ (hierarchy violation).
Ancestor loss sum $\sum_{v \in \text{Anc}(y)} [-\log P(v \mid \text{pa}(v))]$ Penalizes wrong-branch mistakes more heavily than sibling mistakes via shared parent gradients If you drop ancestor terms and only penalize independent local heads without negative sampling, a child classifier $P(\cdot \mid \text{Dog})$ never sees non-Dog images during training and fires wildly on trucks at test time.
Exact path product vs. greedy top-down argmax Finds the leaf or branch maximizing true joint probability $P(y)$ If you pick $\arg\max$ at Level 1 and prune the runner-up branch, a $51\%$ split branch whose children score $26\%$ and $25\%$ beats a $49\%$ branch with a single $49\%$ leaf!

3. Tree-distance cost, LCA metrics ($hF_1$) & selective backoff

Why does hierarchy matter even when the model makes an unavoidable mistake? Because mistaking a Pug for its sibling Husky (Lowest Common Ancestor $= \text{Dog}$, tree distance $d_T = 2$ edges) is far less harmful than mistaking a Pug for a DumpTruck ($\text{LCA} = \text{Root}$, $d_T = 6$ edges).

Flat accuracy scores both mistakes as $0$. Hierarchical Precision and Recall ($hP, hR, hF_1$) instead grade the full ancestor path $\text{Anc}(\hat{y})$ against the true non-root ancestor path $\text{Anc}(y)$:

$$hP = \frac{|\text{Anc}(y) \cap \text{Anc}(\hat{y})|}{|\text{Anc}(\hat{y})|}, \qquad hR = \frac{|\text{Anc}(y) \cap \text{Anc}(\hat{y})|}{|\text{Anc}(y)|}, \qquad \hat{y}_{\text{CRM}} = \arg\min_{\hat{y}} \sum_{y} P(y)\, C(y, \hat{y})$$
Here $C(y, \hat{y})$ is the tree-distance cost matrix (e.g. $d_T(y, \hat{y})$). Picking $\hat{y}_{\text{CRM}}$ (Conditional Risk Minimization) chooses the node with the lowest expected tree-distance penalty—or backs off to an internal parent node $u$ when $P(u) \ge \tau_{\text{parent}}$ while no leaf exceeds $\tau_{\text{leaf}}$.

Worked trace of Hierarchical Precision, Recall & Backoff on $\text{Animal} \to \text{Dog} \to \text{Pug}$:

True label $y = \text{Pug}$ has 3 non-root ancestors: $\text{Anc}(y) = \{\text{Animal},\; \text{Dog},\; \text{Pug}\}$ ($|\text{Anc}(y)| = 3$). Compare three predictions:
• Case 1 (Cross-branch mistake $\hat{y} = \text{Truck}$): $\text{Anc}(\hat{y}) = \{\text{Vehicle},\; \text{Auto},\; \text{Truck}\}$. Intersection $= \emptyset$ ($0$ shared nodes) $\implies hP = 0/3 = \mathbf{0\%}$, $hR = 0/3 = \mathbf{0\%}$.
• Case 2 (Sibling mistake $\hat{y} = \text{Husky}$): $\text{Anc}(\hat{y}) = \{\text{Animal},\; \text{Dog},\; \text{Husky}\}$ ($|\text{Anc}(\hat{y})| = 3$). Shared ancestors $= \{\text{Animal},\; \text{Dog}\}$ ($2$ nodes) $\implies hP = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}}$, $hR = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}}$.

Both predicted nodes are correct ($|\text{Anc}(y) \cap \text{Anc}(\hat{y})| = 2$), so hierarchical recall is $hR = \frac{2}{3}$.
