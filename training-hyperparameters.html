Training Hyperparameters: Why Runs Explode, Stall, or Overfit

Every deep learning practitioner has lived through the same three training headaches: you launch a run and watch the loss explode into NaN within fifty steps; you lower the step size and watch the loss plateau for hours without making progress; or you finally get training loss down near zero only to watch validation loss climb steadily upward.

Those failures happen because training hyperparameters are not independent dials you can tune one at a time. The learning rate ($\eta$) sets how far you jump along the downhill slope, the batch size ($B$) controls how noisy that slope estimate is (with gradient accumulation $K$ letting a small GPU simulate a large effective batch $B_{\text{eff}} = B_{\text{micro}} \times K$), and warmup + gradient clipping keep early AdamW steps from flying off steep loss walls before the optimizer's variance estimate $v_t$ has stabilized.

1. The Step-Size Cliff: Learning Rate ($\eta$), Batch Size ($B$) & Accumulation ($K$)

Why doesn't a bigger learning rate $\eta$ simply reach the bottom of the loss valley faster? Watch a single weight $w$ descend a simple parabola $\mathcal{L}(w) = w^2$, whose minimum sits at $w^* = 0$, slope is $g = 2w$, and second derivative (curvature $H$) is $2$. Under gradient descent $w_{t+1} = w_t - \eta g_t = w_t(1 - 2\eta)$, there is a hard stability ceiling at $\eta = 2 / H = 1.0$:

Worked Example (Tracing 3 learning-rate regimes from $w_0 = 4$, initial loss $4^2 = 16$, slope $g_0 = 8$):

• Steady descent ($\eta = 0.25$): $w_1 = 4 - 0.25(8) = \mathbf{2} \to w_2 = \mathbf{1} \to w_3 = \mathbf{0.5}$ (halves cleanly every step).
• Wall-to-wall bounce ($\eta = 1.0 = 2/H$): $w_1 = 4 - 1.0(8) = \mathbf{-4} \to w_2 = \mathbf{+4}$ (jumps across the valley to the opposite wall at the exact same loss $16$ forever).
• Diverging explosion ($\eta = 1.5 > 2/H$): $w_1 = 4 - 1.5(8) = \mathbf{-8} \to w_2 = \mathbf{+16} \to w_3 = \mathbf{-32}$ (doubles in magnitude every step until float32 overflows to NaN).

In real training, we don't know the exact slope across millions of examples; we estimate it from a mini-batch of $B$ examples. Averaging $B$ independent sample gradients cuts gradient standard deviation by $\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}$. If your GPU only fits a micro-batch $B_{\text{micro}} = 2$ in memory, gradient accumulation runs $K$ forward/backward micro-steps and averages their gradients before calling the optimizer—giving the exact same update as an effective batch size $B_{\text{eff}} = B_{\text{micro}} \times K$. And whenever you multiply $B_{\text{eff}}$ by $4\times$ for a fixed number of epochs, you take $4\times$ fewer optimizer steps per epoch—so you must scale $\eta$ up (linearly $\times 4$ for SGD, or by $\sqrt{4} = 2\times$ for AdamW) so the model doesn't under-train.

On the parabola $\mathcal{L}(w) = w^2$ starting at $w_0 = 5$ (where slope $g_0 = 2w_0 = 10$), you set $\eta = 0.8$. Where does $w_1 = w_0 - \eta g_0$ land, and how does it behave?

2. Warmup, Cosine Decay, Clipping & Why AdamW Decouples Weight Decay

Why did AdamW replace plain SGD as the default optimizer for Transformers? Because in a deep network, a rare token embedding might get a gradient of $0.001$ while an output layer gets a gradient of $10.0$. AdamW keeps two per-weight moving averages: momentum $m_t$ ($\beta_1 \approx 0.9$, smoothing direction) and uncentered variance $v_t$ ($\beta_2 \approx 0.999$, smoothing squared gradient $g_t^2$). Dividing each weight's update by $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ normalizes every parameter by its own typical gradient scale:

$$w_{t+1} = w_t - \eta_t \left(\frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} + \lambda w_t\right)$$

Look at the two pieces inside that update. First, during steps $1\text{–}100$, the variance tracker $v_t$ has only seen a tiny handful of batches, so $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ can be wildly miscalibrated; linear warmup ramps $\eta_t$ gently from $0$ up to $\eta_{\max}$ over the first $1\text{–}5\%$ of steps while $v_t$ stabilizes, before a cosine schedule decays $\eta_t$ smoothly down to settle into a narrow minimum. Meanwhile, gradient norm clipping acts as an emergency circuit breaker at any step: whenever the global norm $\|g\|_2$ spikes above threshold $c$ (say, $c = 1.0$), it rescales the entire gradient vector by $\frac{c}{\|g\|_2}$, capping the step length to $c$ without changing its direction.

Second, notice why AdamW puts the weight decay term $+\lambda w_t$ outside the $\frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}}$ fraction instead of adding classic L2 regularization $\frac{\lambda}{2}w^2$ to the loss. If you add $\lambda w$ into the gradient $g_t$ before dividing by $\sqrt{v_t}$, any weight with large historical gradients ($\sqrt{v_t} = 10$) has its regularization penalty divided by $10$, whereas a quiet weight ($\sqrt{v_t} = 1$) shrinks $10\times$ faster! Decoupling $\lambda w_t$ guarantees every weight shrinks by the exact same fraction $\eta_t \lambda$ per step regardless of its gradient history.

Your gradient clipping threshold is set to $c = 2.0$. On a bad batch, a 2-parameter gradient vector jumps to $g = [6,\; 8]$ (so its Euclidean norm is $\|g\|_2 = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$). What is the clipped gradient vector $g_{\text{clipped}} = g \cdot \frac{c}{\|g\|_2}$?

Two weights both have current value $w_A = w_B = 10$, learning rate $\eta = 0.1$, and decay coefficient $\lambda = 0.1$. Weight A has a noisy gradient history ($\sqrt{\hat{v}_A} = 10$) while Weight B has a quiet history ($\sqrt{\hat{v}_B} = 1$). Under classic L2 regularization inside Adam (where $\lambda w$ is divided by $\sqrt{\hat{v}}$) versus decoupled AdamW (where decay is $-\eta \lambda w$), how much does the decay term shrink Weight A in one step?

3. Hyperparameter Symptom & Fix Cheat Sheet

Training Symptom Root Cause Primary Hyperparameter Fix
Loss explodes to NaN in steps $1\text{–}100$ Unwarmed AdamW variance $v_t \approx 0$ or $\eta > 2/\text{curvature}$ Add linear warmup ($3\text{–}5\%$ of steps), enable grad clipping ($c=1.0$), or lower peak $\eta$
Loss oscillates wildly mid-training Mini-batch $B$ too small or $\eta$ too high for sharp valley walls Increase $B_{\text{eff}}$ via accumulation $K$ and decay $\eta_t$ with a cosine schedule
GPU runs out of VRAM on batch size $B$ Forward activations exceed memory Halve $B_{\text{micro}}$ and double accumulation $K$ to keep $B_{\text{eff}} = B_{\text{micro}} \times K$ unchanged
Train loss $\to 0$, Val loss climbs upward Weights growing unchecked to memorize training noise Increase AdamW decoupled weight decay $\lambda$ (e.g. $0.01 \to 0.1$) and stop early
