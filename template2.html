Softmax Temperature and Numerical Overflow

TL;DR

  • Problem: Neural networks output unbounded logits, which must be turned into probabilities without overflowing float32.
  • Core mechanism: Softmax divides $e^{(z_i - m)/T}$ by the sum of all terms, where subtracting $m = \max(z)$ prevents overflow and temperature $T$ controls sharpness.
  • Where it breaks: Naive exp(z) turns into NaN once any logit exceeds $88.72$, and setting $T \to 0$ collapses entropy to zero.

2. Shift by the max, then scale by temperature

Subtracting a constant $m$ from every logit leaves softmax unchanged mathematically because $e^{-m}$ cancels in numerator and denominator. Choosing $m = \max(z)$ guarantees every exponent is $\le 0$, so $e^{z_i - m}$ stays in $(0, 1]$ and never overflows.

Concrete trace before the general formula: Take $z = [100, 99, 98]$ at $T = 1.0$.

1. Find $m = \max(100, 99, 98) = 100$.
2. Shift: $z - m = [0, -1, -2]$.
3. Exponentiate: $[e^0, e^{-1}, e^{-2}] = [1.000, 0.368, 0.135]$, sum $= 1.503$.
4. Normalize: $p = [0.665, 0.245, 0.090]$.

$$p_i = \frac{\exp\!\left(\frac{z_i - m}{T}\right)}{\sum_{j} \exp\!\left(\frac{z_j - m}{T}\right)}, \quad m = \max_j z_j$$

Reconstruct the Stability Rule

Without scrolling up, write in one sentence why subtracting $\max(z)$ fixes overflow without changing the output probabilities.

Canonical answer: Subtracting $m = \max(z)$ multiplies both numerator and denominator by $e^{-m}$, which cancels out in the ratio, while keeping every exponent $\le 0$ so $e^{z_i - m} \in (0, 1]$ never exceeds the float32 limit ($88.72$).
