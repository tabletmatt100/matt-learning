Softmax Temperature and Numerical Overflow

The final layer of a language model does not output probabilities directly. It outputs raw, unbounded scores called logits—numbers that can be $-15$, $+4.2$, or $+100$. To sample the next word or compute cross-entropy loss, we have to turn that vector of arbitrary scores into a valid probability distribution where every entry is positive and the whole vector adds up to $1$.

The standard tool for that job is softmax, which exponentiates each score ($e^{z_i}$) and divides by the sum. On paper, exponentiation looks harmless. On an actual GPU running float32 arithmetic, feeding raw logits into $e^{z_i}$ is a ticking time bomb that crashes training runs with NaN the moment a single logit drifts past $88.72$.

Fixing that crash takes a one-line algebraic shift—subtracting the maximum logit before exponentiating—while a single divisor called temperature ($T$) lets us reshape how sharply or evenly probability mass spreads across competing tokens.

2. Shift by the Max, Then Scale by Temperature

Why can we fix the overflow without changing the math? If you subtract any constant $m$ from every logit, you multiply every numerator and every term in the denominator by $e^{-m}$. Because $e^{-m} / e^{-m} = 1$, the constant cancels out completely. Choosing $m = \max(z)$ guarantees that the largest shifted logit is $0$ and every other shifted logit is negative, so $e^{z_i - m}$ stays safely inside $(0, 1]$.

Tracing max-shifted softmax on $z = [100, 99, 98]$ at $T = 1.0$:

1. Pull out the maximum score: $m = \max(100, 99, 98) = 100$.
2. Subtract $m$ from every entry: $z - m = [0, -1, -2]$.
3. Exponentiate the shifted scores: $[e^0, e^{-1}, e^{-2}] \approx [1.000, 0.368, 0.135]$, which sum to $1.503$.
4. Divide by the sum to get valid probabilities: $p = [0.665, 0.245, 0.090]$.

$$p_i = \frac{\exp\!\left(\frac{z_i - m}{T}\right)}{\sum_{j} \exp\!\left(\frac{z_j - m}{T}\right)}, \quad m = \max_j z_j$$

Once the overflow is tamed, dividing the shifted logits by temperature $T$ controls how aggressively softmax favors the leader. A low temperature ($T < 1$) stretches the gaps between scores before exponentiation, pushing almost all mass onto the top token. A high temperature ($T > 1$) compresses the gaps toward zero, flattening the distribution toward uniform.
