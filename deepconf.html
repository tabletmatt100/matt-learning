DeepConf: Filtering & Early-Stopping Reasoning Traces by Local Confidence

TL;DR

  • The flaw in Majority Voting ($\text{cons@}N$): Sampling $N = 512$ parallel reasoning traces gives a rambling trace that guessed at the end the exact same $1$ vote as a clean derivation, and wastes millions of tokens generating broken paths to completion.
  • Local worst-window score: DeepConf measures token confidence $C_i$ from the top-$k$ log-probabilities, averages $C_i$ over a sliding window of $n$ tokens (group confidence $C_{G_i}$), and scores the trace by its worst window $C_{\min} = \min_i C_{G_i}$ so a brief reasoning collapse cannot hide inside a long trace average.
  • Offline filter + Online early stop: Offline keeps the top $\eta\%$ traces by $C_{\min}$ for a confidence-weighted vote. Online sets a cutoff $s$ from $N_{\text{init}} = 16$ warmup traces, kills any new trace the instant a window drops below $s$, and stops sampling once leading vote share $\beta \ge \tau$ (cutting up to $84.7\%$ of tokens).
  • Where it breaks: Confidence measures internal certainty, not truth. If a model makes a fluent, high-confidence error, DeepConf keeps it and throws out hesitant-but-correct traces.

$C_i$ Token confidence at step $i$: average negative log-prob of the top-$k$ candidates $-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k \log P_i(j)$ (higher $=$ surer)

2. Token confidence ($C_i$) & sliding-window group confidence ($C_{G_i}$)

Why not measure confidence using the probability of the single winning token? Because inference servers (vLLM) already return the top-$k$ log-probabilities at every step $i$, and looking at the top-$k$ candidates tells you how strongly the model ruled out the runners-up.

$$C_i = -\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k} \log P_i(j), \qquad C_{G_i} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{t = i - n + 1}^{i} C_t, \qquad C_{\min}(t) = \min_{i} C_{G_i}$$

$-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k \log P_i(j)$ Measures how aggressively runner-up tokens ($j=2\dots k$) are suppressed If you only used the top-1 token ($k=1$), $-\log P_i(1)$ sits near $0$ whenever $P_i(1) \ge 0.8$, losing sensitivity to whether rank-2 is $0.19$ (two-way fork) or $0.001$.
Window mean $C_{G_i} = \frac{1}{n}\sum C_t$ Smooths single-token spikes over $n = 2048$ tokens If $n = 1$ (no window), a single valid branching word ("Alternatively") kills the entire trace. If $n = L$ (whole trace), a 300-token collapse is diluted by $10{,}000$ easy tokens.
Trace minimum $\min_i C_{G_i}$ Flags the single weakest reasoning link; decreases monotonically as tokens stream If replaced by average $C_{\text{avg}}$ or tail confidence, you cannot early-stop online because future tokens could raise the average back above threshold $s$.

3. Offline filtering vs. online early stopping

Once every trace has a worst-window score $C_{\min}$, DeepConf uses it in two modes:

  1. Offline (Filter top $\eta\%$, then weight): Sort completed traces by $C_{\min}$, keep only the top $\eta\%$ (DeepConf-low keeps $\eta = 10\%$; DeepConf-high keeps $\eta = 90\%$), and give each surviving trace a vote weight equal to its confidence score: $$V(a) = \sum_{t \in \text{Top }\eta\%} C_{\min}(t)\,\mathbb{1}[a_t = a], \qquad \hat{a} = \arg\max_a V(a)$$ Why filter instead of just weighting all $N$ traces? Because confidence scores on a question cluster in a narrow band (e.g. $10$ to $14$). Pure weighting gives a $14$-score trace only $1.4\times$ the vote of a $10$-score trace, so four confused traces still outvote three clean ones. Filtering sets the bottom traces' weight to zero.
  2. Online (Warmup threshold $s$ + instant kill + consensus $\beta$): Generate $N_{\text{init}} = 16$ full warmup traces and set cutoff $s$ to the $(100-\eta)$-th percentile of their $C_{\min}$ scores. For every subsequent trace, the instant any sliding window $C_{G_i} < s$, terminate generation immediately (since $\min_i C_{G_i}$ can never climb back above $s$). Stop launching new traces early once the leading answer holds weighted vote share $\beta = V(\hat{a}) / \sum_a V(a) \ge \tau$ ($\tau = 0.95$).
