DeepConf: Filtering & Early-Stopping Reasoning Traces by Local Confidence

When you want a reasoning language model to solve a hard math or coding problem, the standard way to spend extra test-time compute is Self-Consistency ($\text{cons@}N$): sample $N = 64$ or $512$ independent chain-of-thought reasoning traces in parallel (parallel thinking), extract the final boxed answer from each trace, and hold a majority vote.

That simple vote has two glaring blind spots. First, it gives every completed trace the exact same $1$ vote—even if one trace derived its answer with rock-solid certainty from start to finish while another trace got lost halfway through, guessed randomly across a three-way fork, and stumbled onto a popular wrong number. Second, it runs all $512$ traces to the very end, burning millions of tokens on dead-end chains of thought that already went off the rails at token $400$.

Why not just weight each trace by its average token probability across the whole generation? Because a $10{,}000$-token trace is mostly routine algebra and formatting boilerplate; a fatal $200$-token stretch of confusion gets washed out when you average across the entire trace. DeepConf fixes this by sliding a local window across the trace and scoring the entire chain of thought by its single weakest window ($C_{\min}$)—letting you filter out shaky traces offline or kill them mid-stream the instant a local window dips below a warmup cutoff.

2. Measuring Token Confidence From Top-$k$ Runners-Up

How should we measure the model's certainty at a single token step $i$? If you only look at the top-1 winning token's probability $P_i(1)$, its logarithm $-\log P_i(1)$ barely moves whenever $P_i(1)$ is above $0.80$, hiding whether the runner-up token is sitting at $0.19$ (a tense two-way fork) or $0.0001$ (total certainty). On the other hand, computing full Shannon entropy requires summing over all $100{,}000+$ tokens in the vocabulary, which inference servers like vLLM don't return over the wire.

DeepConf uses the top-$k$ log-probabilities ($k = 5$ or $20$) that vLLM already returns for free. It defines token confidence $C_i$ as the average negative log-probability across the top-$k$ candidate tokens at position $i$.

Wait—why does a larger average of $-\log P_i(j)$ mean higher confidence? Because the sum is dominated by the runner-up tokens ($j = 2 \dots k$). When the model is certain of its choice, it crushes the runner-up probabilities down near zero (like $2^{-6} = \frac{1}{64}$), and the negative logarithm of a tiny fraction is a large positive number ($-\log_2(\frac{1}{64}) = +6\text{ bits}$). When the model is torn between several candidates, the runners-up keep large probabilities (like $\frac{1}{4}$), so their $-\log_2 P$ values stay small ($+2\text{ bits}$).

Tracing token confidence in base-2 bits ($k = 3$ candidates, using $-\log_2 P$):

• Position A (Certain model): Top-3 candidate probabilities are $P_A = [\frac{1}{2},\; \frac{1}{16},\; \frac{1}{64}]$.
Taking $-\log_2 P_A$ turns those fractions into exact integer bits: $[1,\; 4,\; 6]$.
Averaging across the $k = 3$ candidates gives $C_A = \frac{1 + 4 + 6}{3} = \frac{11}{3} \approx \mathbf{3.67\text{ bits}}$.

• Position B (Torn / hedging model): Top-3 candidate probabilities are $P_B = [\frac{1}{4},\; \frac{1}{4},\; \frac{1}{8}]$.
Their negative base-2 logs are $-\log_2 P_B = [2,\; 2,\; 3]$.

$$C_i = -\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k} \log P_i(j), \qquad C_{G_i} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{t = i - n + 1}^{i} C_t, \qquad C_{\min}(t) = \min_{i} C_{G_i}$$
Averaging $C_t$ over a sliding window of $n = 2048$ tokens (group confidence $C_{G_i}$) prevents a single legitimate transition word like "Alternatively," from looking like a collapse, while taking the minimum window $C_{\min} = \min_i C_{G_i}$ catches any sustained stretch of guessing.

3. Why You Must Filter Before Weighting & How Online Early Stopping Works

Once every reasoning trace has a worst-window score $C_{\min}$, why not simply give all $N$ traces a vote weighted by $C_{\min}$? Because on any given prompt, the numerical values of $C_{\min}$ across traces tend to cluster in a fairly narrow positive band—say, between $5$ and $10$. Under an unfiltered weighted vote, a rock-solid trace scoring $10$ only gets twice the vote of a confused trace scoring $5$, so three rambling traces that guessed the same wrong distraction ($5 + 6 + 7 = 18$) will still outvote one clean derivation ($10$).

DeepConf solves this in offline mode by sorting all completed traces by $C_{\min}$, keeping only the top $\eta\%$ (DeepConf-low keeps the top $\eta = 10\%$; DeepConf-high keeps the top $\eta = 90\%$), and setting the vote weight of every trace below that cutoff to zero:

$$V(a) = \sum_{t \in \text{Top }\eta\%} C_{\min}(t)\,\mathbb{1}[a_t = a], \qquad \hat{a} = \arg\max_a V(a)$$

Even better, scoring traces by a minimum over windows ($\min_i C_{G_i}$) unlocks an exact online early-stopping rule. Notice a simple mathematical property of a running minimum: as a trace streams out new tokens, its running worst window $\min_{i \le t} C_{G_i}$ can only stay flat or go down—it can never climb back up. So DeepConf first runs $N_{\text{init}} = 16$ full warmup traces to set a threshold $s$ at the $(100-\eta)$-th percentile of their $C_{\min}$ scores. From trace $17$ onward, the very instant any sliding window $C_{G_i}$ dips below $s$, vLLM kills that trace mid-sentence. And once the leading answer holds a confidence-weighted vote share $\beta = V(\hat{a}) / \sum_a V(a) \ge \tau$ ($\tau = 0.95$), it stops launching new traces altogether—cutting up to $84.7\%$ of generated tokens.

4. Where DeepConf Breaks & Hands-On Practice

DeepConf has one fundamental limitation: log-probabilities measure internal model certainty, not objective truth. If a model suffers from a fluent, high-confidence misconception on a trick question—while the few traces that catch the trap hesitate and second-guess themselves—aggressive filtering (DeepConf-low, keeping only the top $10\%$) will throw out the hesitant correct traces and lock onto the confident mistake. That is why DeepConf-high (keeping the top $90\%$ and only trimming the bottom $10\%$ of clear collapses) is the safer default when you haven't calibrated on a validation set.
