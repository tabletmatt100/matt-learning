16. Spaced Repetition Systems (SRS) From Scratch: Forgetting Curves, SM-2 & FSRS Math

Why rereading notes wastes 80% of your study time, how exponential and power-law forgetting curves schedule reviews at the exact moment you are 10% likely to forget, and how the live SRS deck in this library works.

TL;DR
  • Memory has two separate numbers, not one: Retrievability $R(t) \in [0, 1]$ is the probability you can recall a fact right now (which decays every day), while Stability $S$ (in days) is how long it takes for $R(t)$ to drop to $90\%$.
  • Reviewing too early wastes effort: Because of the spacing effect, recalling a card when $R \approx 99\%$ (1 hour later) barely increases Stability $S$. Recalling it when $R \approx 90\%$ (right before you forget) triggers high desirable difficulty and multiplies $S$ by $2\times\text{–}3.5\times$.
  • Classic SM-2 vs. modern FSRS: SM-2 (Anki's classic algorithm) multiplies the previous interval by an Ease Factor $EF \ge 1.30$, which can trap cards in Ease Hell after a few rough days. FSRS (the DSR model) separates item Difficulty $D$ from Stability $S$ and models power-law forgetting.

2. The Forgetting Curve: Retrievability $R(t)$ vs. Stability $S$

In 1885, Hermann Ebbinghaus memorized nonsense syllables and measured how rapidly recall decayed when he waited $t$ days before testing himself. Modern memory models formalize his forgetting curve using two variables:

  • Retrievability $R(t) \in [0, 1]$: the probability that you can recall the card $t$ days after your last review. At the moment of review ($t = 0$), $R(0) = 1.00$ ($100\%$).
  • Stability $S > 0$ (measured in days): the time it takes for Retrievability to fall to your target threshold $R^* = 0.90$ ($90\%$).

If we calibrate the exponential decay curve so that $R(S) = 0.90$ when $t = S$ days, the base of the exponent is simply $0.9$:

$$R(t) = 0.9^{\,t / S} = \exp\!\left(\ln(0.9)\,\frac{t}{S}\right) \approx e^{-0.10536\, t / S}$$
Tap symbols: $R(t)$ (retrievability at day $t$) · $S$ (stability in days where $R=90\%$)

Worked numerical example (Exponential vs. Power-Law Decay):

Suppose you learn the LoRA parameter compression formula today and its initial Stability is $S = 4\text{ days}$.

  1. After $t = 2\text{ days}$: $R(2) = 0.9^{2/4} = 0.9^{0.5} \approx 0.9487$ ($94.9\%$ chance of recall). Reviewing now is slightly premature.
  2. After $t = 4\text{ days}$ ($t = S$): $R(4) = 0.9^{4/4} = 0.9000$ ($90.0\%$ chance of recall). If your target retention is $R^* = 90\%$, day $4$ is the exact day the scheduler surfaces the card.
  3. After $t = 12\text{ days}$ ($t = 3S$, if you skipped reviews): $R(12) = 0.9^{12/4} = 0.9^3 = 0.7290$ ($72.9\%$ chance of recall).

Suppose you review the card on day $4$ and recall it successfully. That single active retrieval multiplies its stability from $S_1 = 4\text{d}$ to $S_2 = 10\text{d}$ ($2.5\times$ flatter decay). Now it takes $10$ days—not $4$—for $R(t)$ to drop back to $90\%$.

3. How Classic SM-2 Schedules Cards (and Why "Ease Hell" Happens)

In 1987, Piotr Wozniak designed SM-2 for SuperMemo 2. Variations of SM-2 still power Anki's default scheduler and the 4-button scheduler built into matt-learning. Every card stores three numbers: repetition streak $n$, current interval $I$ (days), and an Ease Factor $EF$ (initialized to $2.50$).

$$I_1 = 1\text{d}, \qquad I_2 = 4\text{d}, \qquad I_n = \text{round}(I_{n-1} \cdot EF) \quad (n \ge 3)$$ $$EF' = \max\!\Big(1.30,\;\; EF + \Delta EF(\text{grade})\Big)$$
4-button $\Delta EF$: Again $\to -0.20$ (resets $n=0, I=1$) · Hard $\to -0.15$ ($I \gets 1.2 I$) · Good $\to +0.00$ · Easy $\to +0.15$ ($I \gets 1.3 \cdot I \cdot EF'$)
The Structural Flaw in SM-2: "Ease Hell"

Notice the asymmetry in $\Delta EF$ above: pressing Again ($-0.20$) or Hard ($-0.15$) lowers $EF$, while pressing Good leaves $EF$ completely unchanged ($+0.00$). Only pressing Easy ($+0.15$) raises $EF$.

If a card is tricky when you first learn it and you press Again or Hard four times, its Ease Factor hits the floor $EF = 1.30$. Even after you master the concept and press Good ten times in a row, $EF$ stays stuck at $1.30$—growing intervals at a crawl ($1 \to 4 \to 5 \to 7 \to 9\text{d}$) instead of ($1 \to 4 \to 10 \to 25 \to 63\text{d}$). This trap is called Ease Hell.

4. How Modern FSRS (DSR Model) Fixes SM-2

FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler, Jarrett Ye 2022–2024, built on Wozniak's DSR model) replaces SM-2's heuristic Ease Factor with three explicit variables learned from hundreds of millions of real review logs:

  1. Difficulty $D \in [1, 10]$: How intrinsically hard the card is ($D = 1$ easiest, $D = 10$ hardest). Unlike SM-2's Ease Factor, $D$ updates with mean reversion toward the default difficulty $D_0$, so a card can recover from early stumbles.
  2. Stability $S > 0$: Days until recall probability drops to $90\%$ under a power-law forgetting curve $R(t, S) = \left(1 + \frac{19}{81}\frac{t}{S}\right)^{-0.5}$.
  3. Retrievability $R \in (0, 1]$: Evaluated at the actual elapsed days $\Delta t$ when you review. If you were offline for 10 days on a 4-day card ($R \approx 0.78$) and still remembered it, FSRS rewards that surprise with a much bigger jump in new Stability $S'$:
$$S'_{\text{recall}}(D, S, R) = S \cdot \Big(1 + c \cdot (11 - D) \cdot S^{-w} \cdot \big(e^{\gamma (1 - R)} - 1\big)\Big)$$
Notice $(e^{\gamma(1 - R)} - 1)$: when $R \to 1$ (cramming 5 minutes later), $e^0 - 1 = 0$, so $S' \approx S$ (zero stability gain!). When $R$ is lower, the gain is large.

5. Minimal 35-Line SRS Implementation

Below is the core scheduling logic running in common.js for matt-learning. You can drop this function into any static site or local JSON tool:

View standalone JavaScript SM-2 Scheduler (35 lines) 
function scheduleCard(prev, grade, todayDay) {
  // prev: { reps, interval, ease, lapses } or null for a new card
  var reps     = (prev && prev.reps)     ? prev.reps     : 0;
  var interval = (prev && prev.interval) ? prev.interval : 0;
  var ease     = (prev && prev.ease)     ? prev.ease     : 2.50;
  var lapses   = (prev && prev.lapses)   ? prev.lapses   : 0;

  if (grade === 'again') {
    reps = 0;
    interval = 1;
    ease = Math.max(1.30, Number((ease - 0.20).toFixed(2)));
    lapses += 1;
  } else if (grade === 'hard') {
    reps = Math.max(1, reps);
    interval = (reps === 1 && interval === 0) ? 2 : Math.max(interval + 1, Math.round(interval * 1.2));
    ease = Math.max(1.30, Number((ease - 0.15).toFixed(2)));
  } else if (grade === 'good') {
    reps += 1;
    interval = (reps === 1) ? 1 : (reps === 2) ? 4 : Math.max(interval + 1, Math.round(interval * ease));
  } else if (grade === 'easy') {
    reps += 1;
    ease = Number((ease + 0.15).toFixed(2));
    interval = (reps === 1) ? 4 : (reps === 2) ? 8 : Math.max(interval + 2, Math.round(interval * ease * 1.3));
  }

  return { reps: reps, interval: interval, ease: ease, lapses: lapses, dueDay: todayDay + interval };
}
